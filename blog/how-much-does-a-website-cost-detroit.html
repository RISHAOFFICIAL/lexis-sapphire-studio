How Much Does a Website Cost in Detroit? (2026 Guide)

Website prices in Detroit range from a few hundred dollars to tens of thousands. Here is what actually drives the price, what you should expect at each level, and how to avoid paying for things you do not need.

If you have ever tried to figure out how much a website costs, you have probably noticed something frustrating: nobody gives you a straight answer. One company quotes $500. Another quotes $15,000. Both say they build websites.

So what is the real price? The honest answer is that it depends on what you need. But there are clear price ranges in Detroit, and once you understand what drives the cost, you can make a smart decision instead of guessing.

The short answer

For most Detroit small businesses, a professional custom website costs between $1,000 and $3,000 for the initial build. That covers a multi-page site designed specifically for your business, with your branding, your photos, your services, and everything set up to work on phones and show up on Google.

Simpler sites cost less. More complex sites with online stores, booking systems, or custom features cost more. Here is the full breakdown.

Price ranges: what you get at each level

Under $500: Template and DIY

At this price, you are getting a template. Someone takes a pre-made design, swaps in your business name and a few photos, and calls it done. Or you build it yourself on a platform like Wix or Squarespace.

This can work if you just need something basic online and you are willing to do the work yourself. But there are real limits. Template sites look like every other template site. They are hard to customize. They often load slowly. And they rarely rank well on Google because the content is thin and generic.

Some budget web designers offer $149 or $499 websites. Read the fine print. Those prices usually cover a very basic site with limited pages, and every feature you actually need (booking, galleries, service menus, contact forms) costs extra as an add-on. A $499 site with the add-ons most businesses need (booking form $129, gallery $79, service menu $129, testimonials $59, FAQ section $59, team page $79, advanced contact form $79, business email $59/month) creeps past $2,000 fast, and you still do not have a custom design. By the time you add everything you need, the total is often more than what a custom site would have cost in the first place.

$1,000 to $3,000: Custom small business website

This is the sweet spot for most Detroit small businesses. At this level, you get a website designed specifically for your business, not a template. That means:

  • A design built around your brand, your colors, and your personality
  • Multiple pages covering your services, your story, and your contact info
  • Real photos of your work, not stock images
  • Built to look great on phones, tablets, and computers
  • Set up so Google can find and rank it
  • Connected to your booking system, social media, or online ordering
  • Delivered in about two weeks

At Lexis Sapphire Studio, custom websites start at $1,500. That is the price for a complete, professional site for a Detroit small business, designed from scratch for that specific business.

$3,000 to $10,000: Larger sites and online stores

Once you add e-commerce, a large number of pages, custom booking flows, customer accounts, or integrations with other software, the price goes up. A restaurant with online ordering, a retail shop with a full product catalog, or a service business with a complex scheduling system falls into this range.

The cost goes up because the work goes up. More pages to design, more features to build, more testing to make sure everything works. But for a business that sells online, the website is a sales channel, not just a brochure, so the investment pays back through direct sales.

$10,000 and up: Agency and enterprise

Large agencies in Detroit and elsewhere charge $10,000 to $50,000 or more for websites. At this level, you are paying for a full team: project managers, designers, developers, copywriters, and strategists. That makes sense for a large company with a big budget and complex needs.

For most small businesses, this is overkill. You do not need a team of twelve people to build a website for a salon, a restaurant, or a tire shop. You need one skilled person who understands your business and builds exactly what you need.

What drives the price up or down

Several factors affect what your website will cost:

  • Number of pages. A five-page site costs less than a twenty-page site. More pages means more design and content work.
  • Custom design vs. template. A design built from scratch for your business costs more than a template, but it also looks better, works better, and actually represents your brand.
  • Features. Online booking, e-commerce, photo galleries, quote forms, and customer logins all add complexity and cost.
  • Content. If you provide your own text and photos, the price is lower. If the designer writes your copy and sources your images, that takes more time.
  • Who builds it. A freelancer or small studio charges less than a large agency because they have lower overhead. That does not mean lower quality. It means you are paying for the work, not for the office.

Ongoing costs: what to budget after launch

The build price is a one-time cost. But a website has ongoing costs too, and you should know about them upfront:

  • Domain name: about $10 to $15 per year for a .com address.
  • Hosting: free to about $20 per month, depending on the setup. Many small business sites can run on free hosting with no monthly fee.
  • Updates and maintenance: if you want someone to keep your site current with new services, prices, photos, and seasonal changes, expect $75 to $150 per month for a small business site.

Be careful with companies that charge high monthly fees for basic hosting. Some agencies charge $100 to $200 per month just to keep a simple site online. For most small business websites, that is far more than the actual hosting costs. Ask what the monthly fee covers, and make sure you are not paying a premium for something that costs almost nothing to run.

How to avoid overpaying

A few simple rules:

  • Get the total price in writing before work starts. Know exactly what is included and what costs extra.
  • Ask what happens after launch. Do you own the website? Can you take it to another developer if you want? You should own your site outright once it is paid for.
  • Watch out for add-on pricing. A low headline price with expensive add-ons for basic features is a common trap.
  • Do not pay for features you do not need. A small business website does not need a custom app, a blog network, or enterprise analytics. Keep it focused on what brings you customers.

The bottom line

For most Detroit small businesses, a great custom website costs $1,500 to $3,000 upfront, plus about $10 to $15 per year for the domain and optionally $75 to $150 per month for updates and maintenance. That is a real investment, but it is one that pays for itself when the website brings in new customers month after month.

Think of it this way: if your website brings in just one new customer a month that you would not have gotten otherwise, it has paid for itself within the first year. Everything after that is return on your investment.
