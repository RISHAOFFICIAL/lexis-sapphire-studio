Why Your Detroit Braider Business Needs a Real Website (Not Just a Booking Link)

Your booking page is not a website. If clients can only find you through Instagram DMs, you are losing bookings every single week.

You have the skills. Your knotless braids are clean, your stitch work is sharp, and your clients leave happy. So why does it still feel like you are hustling for every single booking?

Here is the honest answer: most Detroit braiders are running their whole business off an Instagram page and a booking link. That works until it does not. And right now, it is costing you clients you never even meet.

1. Google cannot find a booking link

Think about how a new client finds a braider. She does not scroll Instagram hoping to stumble on your page. She opens Google and types "knotless braids Detroit" or "braider near me" or "stitch braids Detroit."

Google does not rank booking pages. Your Acuity or StyleSeat link is a scheduling tool, not a website. It has no real content for Google to index, no service descriptions, no photos with proper titles, and no location pages. When someone searches for a braider in Detroit, Google shows businesses with real websites. If you do not have one, you are invisible to every person searching that way.

That is a lot of people. Searches for braiders in Detroit happen every day, all day. A real website puts you in front of them. A booking link does not.

2. You are answering the same questions 50 times a day

"How much for medium knotless?" "Do you do boho?" "Where are you located?" "Do I need to bring my own hair?" "How long does it take?"

Sound familiar? Every one of those questions is a DM you have to stop and answer. That is time you could spend braiding, or resting, or doing literally anything else.

A real website answers all of it before anyone messages you. Every style gets its own page or card with a photo, the price, the time it takes, and a book button that goes straight to that style on your booking page. Your policies are right there. Your location is right there. The client shows up already knowing what she wants and what it costs. You stop being a customer service desk and get back to being a braider.

3. A booking link looks like every other booking link

Open any braider's booking page. Then open another one. They look the same. Same layout, same tiny thumbnails, same plain list of services. Nothing about it says "this is my brand."

Your work is not generic. Your website should not be either. A real website shows your personality, your style, your best work in full size, and your reviews from real clients. When a potential client lands on a beautiful site that feels like you, she trusts you before she ever messages you. When she lands on the same plain booking page as everyone else, you are just another option in a long list.

4. Instagram can disappear. Your website cannot

This one is uncomfortable, but it is real. Instagram accounts get hacked, shadowbanned, or shut down with no warning. If your entire business lives on one platform, one bad day can wipe out your bookings.

A website is yours. Nobody can take it down because of an algorithm change. It works 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, whether you are posting or not. Clients can find you, see your work, and book an appointment at 2 a.m. while you are asleep. That is not a nice extra. That is a business that keeps running when you are not.

5. You can actually show your work properly

Your Instagram feed buries your best work. A client has to scroll through months of posts to find the style she wants. Half the time she gives up and DMs you instead, which puts you right back to answering questions one by one.

On your own website, every style has its own spot with a big, clear photo. Knotless, stitch, feed-ins, boho, locs, all organized so a client can find exactly what she wants in seconds. You can feature your signature styles up front, run a seasonal sale, and show off new work without it getting lost in a feed.

What a braider website should have

Not every website is built the same. Here is what actually matters for a braiding business:

  • Every service with its own photo, price, and time. No guessing, no DMs needed.
  • A book button on every style that takes the client straight to that service on your booking page.
  • Real photos of your work, big and clear, not tiny thumbnails.
  • Your policies (deposits, cancellations, late arrivals) where clients can read them before they book.
  • Your location and contact info where people can actually find it.
  • Google-friendly pages so people searching for a Detroit braider find you.
  • A design that looks like you, not a template that looks like everyone else.

The bottom line

A booking link is a tool. A website is a business. If you are serious about growing your braiding business in Detroit, you need both, and they need to work together. Your website brings clients in and answers their questions. Your booking page closes the appointment.

The braiders who get this are the ones who stop chasing clients and start having clients come to them. That is the difference a real website makes.
