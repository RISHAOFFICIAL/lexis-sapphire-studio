New album Velvet Hours, out September 18, 2026

The voice of the east side, at golden hour.

Maya Sol sings slow songs for long drives, Sunday mornings, and nights that run past midnight. Detroit made the voice. The whole country is starting to hear it.

Maya Sol is a 27-year-old R&B singer from Detroit's east side, and Velvet Hours is the record she spent four years writing. Twelve songs about home, leaving, staying, and the hour of the day when the light turns gold on Chandler Park. It is her third project and her first on Solstice Records.

The lead single, Golden Hour, passed 11 million streams in its first six weeks. The Velvet Hours Tour opens March 6, 2027 at The Fillmore Detroit, ten cities, one month, and the homecoming show is already sold out.

The story

Raised on the east side.

2019First open mic at seventeen, three songs and a borrowed guitar.
2021First paid gig, a Tuesday night set at Baker's Keyboard Lounge, age nineteen.
2022First Light, recorded at home and released independently.
2024Sold out Saint Andrew's Hall, 1,000 people. Night Drives EP follows, and Solstice Records signs her that fall.
2026Velvet Hours, her third project and her first full band record.

Maya Sol grew up on a brick street near Chandler Park, on Detroit's east side, in a house where the radio was never off. She sang in her grandmother's church choir before she could read the hymn book, and she wrote her first song at twelve, on the back of a spelling test. She still has it.

For three years she worked the counter at a record shop on Woodward Avenue, listening to everything that came through the door and saving for studio time. Her first paid gig came at nineteen, a Tuesday night set at Baker's Keyboard Lounge, forty minutes, twelve people in the room, and she sang like it was a thousand.

First Light, her 2022 debut, was recorded in a home studio and mixed at a kitchen table. It found its audience slowly, one shared link at a time, and Corner Store Roses is still the song the crowd sings back the loudest. The Night Drives EP followed in 2024, and by that fall she had sold out Saint Andrew's Hall and signed with Solstice Records.

Velvet Hours is the record she spent four years writing. Twelve songs about home, leaving, staying, and the hour when the light turns gold over the east side. Porch Light was written on her grandmother's porch in a single evening. Letter to Eastside took two years. She says both of those facts are in the songs if you listen close enough.

She still lives in Detroit, three blocks from the house she grew up in. She still sings at the church some Sundays. The tour bus leaves in March.
