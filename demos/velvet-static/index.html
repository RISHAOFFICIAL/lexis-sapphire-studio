The Band

Four people who met in Corktown and never left. Every song is written in the same room, usually loud, usually late.

Mara Voss

Lead Vocals, Rhythm Guitar

Grew up singing in her grandmother's church choir on the east side. Writes most of the songs at her kitchen table in Corktown.

Andre "Dre" Calloway

Lead Guitar

Started on his uncle's beat-up Strat at 13. Ran sound at DIY shows across the Cass Corridor before joining the band.

June Okafor

Bass, Backing Vocals

Classically trained on upright bass, switched to electric in college. Her harmonies are the band's secret weapon.

Marcus "Mars" Webb

Drums

Learned drums in the marching band at Cass Tech. Keeps the pocket deep and the tempos honest.

Music

Three albums in five years, all recorded within ten miles of where the band rehearses. 33 songs, zero filler, one storage unit.

Neon Rust

2021 - Corktown Records - 10 Tracks

The debut, recorded in 9 days at a studio in Hamtramck. Sold out its first 500 vinyl pressing in three weeks. Standouts: "Neon Rust", "Cass Avenue", "Porch Light".

Half-Light Motel

2023 - Static House Records - 12 Tracks

Written on the road during their first headline tour, 24 cities in 31 days. The one that put them on the map. Standouts: "Half-Light Motel", "Room 9", "Checkout Time".

Static Bloom

2025 - Static House Records - 11 Tracks

The latest. 4.2 million streams in its first year and the biggest shows of their career. Standouts: "Power Lines", "Copperhead Summer", "Rust Belt Heart".

Bio

The Velvet Static formed in Corktown in the spring of 2019, when Mara Voss and Dre Calloway met at an open mic and realized they owned the same three records and the same stubborn idea: a band that sounded like Detroit felt. Loud, warm, and a little rusted around the edges.

Their first rehearsal was in a rented storage unit on Michigan Avenue, between a stack of tires and a broken jukebox. Their first gig was a basement house show on Bagley Street. Forty people, five dollars at the door, and a set that ran long because nobody wanted to leave.

June Okafor joined in early 2020 after answering a flyer taped up at a record shop on Cass Avenue. Mars Webb came three months later and the lineup has not changed since. Three albums, 312 shows, and 4.2 million streams later, they still rehearse in the same neighborhood and still write every song in the same room.

"We sound like this city," Mara says. "You can hear the factories and the churches in it. We just turned the amps up."
